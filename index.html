★Executive Summary

ValleyShield BC would commence operations from UFV Abbotsford to assist the Fraser Valley in preparing for the next pandemic, and provide services to universities, health authorities and governments throughout Canada. Its three pillars are PathoPulse (early warning), Surge (additional capacity in health services) and Institute (training). Its business arm would be ValleyShield Analytics Inc. Clinical and public-health decisions would still be made by health authorities. The primary reason for this project is that Ottawa has purchased more than $200 million in portable hospitals and was expecting approximately $7 million in storage and maintenance costs for those hospitals in 2025–26 (Duggan, 2025). When there is no emergency, ValleyShield would plan productive after-use of equipment.

Campus Shield is the new sub-project, and has two networked services. CampusRelay utilizes replaceable air and surface sample cartridges which are submitted to a qualified laboratory. A result could indicate if a target pathogen was found in a building, rather than a person's health status. AirLoop, with sensors in the room and recommended software, will adjust the ventilation and filtration within the established safety parameters, and will also evaluate and verify if there is any energy saving potential when the risk indicators are low. UFV would try it first, and if the pilot is a success, up to three BC partner campuses could try it. Later it might be made available to other Canadian institutions. The potential market segment consists of universities and colleges but as of yet no customers are confirmed (Canadian Information Centre for International Credentials [CICIC], n.d.). Only part of the proposed payment would be based on energy savings that need to be verified. The combined service has not yet been tested, although campus environmental sampling has been studied in Canada (Hinz et al., 2024).

Time is one part of the triple constraint. This project would begin in January of 2027 and end December 31 of 2029 - a span of 36 months. I would list the activities as I followed the steps in the class (Renso, 2026), in order, estimate the time required for each, construct the schedule and review delays monthly. Optional work would be dealt with via change control rather than reducing safety requirements if time is limited. The deadline is for the UFV pilot and their up to three BC partner campuses, as well as a package that will be available for deployment throughout Canada later on.

The suggested sequence of events on this Critical Path is: Charter Approval, Baseline Planning, PathoPulse Lab Construction, Wastewater Validation, Integrated Drills and Closing. Detailed scheduling will ascertain which work activities do not have any spare time. Outside dependencies include permits, deliveries and partner agreements. For the lab build, PERT estimates of 10, 12 and 18 months give an expected duration of (10 + 4 × 12 + 18) ÷ 6 = 12.7 months, with about 1.3 months of uncertainty. I would keep a three-month schedule buffer before closing. The steering committee would determine at the charter, laboratory, pilot and final drill stop points whether to continue, correct issues and/or cease funding.

The initial drafts were too many ideas to include, and I made it four parts in one network, earlier. PathoPulse encompasses 60 wastewater locations, up to 600 poultry barns participating, up to 434 care sites (Office of the Seniors Advocate, 2026), the AI Command Centre and Campus Shield. Surge has plans for four modular units with 100 beds each, in Abbotsford, Kelowna and Nanaimo. Institute would provide training to workers and ValleyShield Analytics Inc. would provide services that would be approved. Campus Shield would begin in about 15 UFV buildings and expand to up to three BC partner campuses, if it was successful and agreed upon. These are objectives and NOT confirmed customers.

The six scope processes are planning, collecting requirements, definition of scope, creation of the WBS, validation of scope and controlling scope (Renso, 2026). For each deliverable there will be an acceptance test, which will be clearly stated. Changes should be documented and indicated in writing with the cost of the change and its timing. The project would not be manufacturing vaccines, building a permanent hospital, offering clinical care or keeping track of each student or automatically issuing public-health orders. By 2029 it is also not feasible to get Campus Shield installed on all Canadian campuses. First, we will map out the current surveillance activities of the BCCDC, meaning that ValleyShield will not duplicate the existing activities of BCCDC (BCCDC, n.d.).

The assumed amount invested is $1 billion. The total allocation is given in Table 3, $494.08 million for the project (including $80 million contingency) and $505.92 million for a proposed long-term reserve. Campus Shield takes $24 million from the budget for future expansion in other jurisdictions, and adds that amount to the funding for Campus Shield. Total does not change. Planning, estimating, budgeting and controlling costs (Renso, 2026) are all part of cost management. Figures are early planning estimates and not supplier quotes.

The $15 million was the reported operating cost of Ontario's 59-site wastewater programme for 2023–24, not a quote for ValleyShield's equipment (Sahakyan et al., 2026). New pricing is required for lab instruments, particularly in the wake of Oxford Nanopore Technologies' announcement of the end of PromethION 2 Solo in 2026 (Oxford Nanopore Technologies, 2026). Examples, but not proof, of what UFV can save are older energy-saving projects at Thompson Rivers University (TRU) (Thompson Rivers University [TRU], 2013, 2015). If we take advantage of $40 million of the $80 million contingency, then the expected project expenditures are $454.08 million. If the $505.92 million reserve earns a 4% return, then it would earn about $20.2 million per year – but not for sure. I would look at planned work, completed work and actual costs before approving the next stage.

4Project Stakeholders

ValleyShield is influenced by stakeholders and can influence them (Renso, 2026). Internal stakeholders are the sponsor, the UFV team, researchers and students. Other external groups include: governments, health authorities, BCCDC, First Nations, municipalities, utilities, farmers, care operators, vendors and campus customers.

I would write down what each group needed, the influences and supports. Farmers require practical support and privacy as the Fraser Valley has a high percentage of commercial poultry farms in BC (Canadian Food Inspection Agency [CFIA], 2023). There should be clear costs, and rules on accessing buildings, at universities. The health authorities would require dependable evidence and participation and/or terms of data sharing by First Nations would be determined by First Nations. I would also allocate one point of contact to each key stakeholder in order to avoid issues and queries being lost between teams.

5Three Spheres of Systems Management

Three spheres are business, organization and technology (Renso, 2026). But, at the business level, the question is whether ValleyShield can afford its own services. Organisation asks about collaboration between UFV, universities and public-health agencies. All three are tested by Campus Shield: a sample can be collected in a cartridge; it needs to be processed by a laboratory; Campus staff must understand how a result will affect ventilation; a partner must agree to pay for the re-testing of a sample.

The technology will be using room sensors and software to make recommendations for ventilation changes in the rooms and CampusRelay would collect environmental samples and send them to a lab. Some research on balancing the risk of indoor air and energy has been investigated (Risbeck et al., 2023), but testing our own system is required. The AI Command Centre will consolidate the building, wastewater and farm results. The sequencing of pathogens has been an area of research that has also been looked into in the light of AI (Gauthier et al., 2025). Trained staff would review results and approve major actions; the system would not diagnose students or make public-health decisions. I would do a feasibility study to see how the technology would run, the campus would be able to sustain the technology and the cost of the service was reasonable before expanding Campus Shield.

6Four Frames of Organizations: UFV

The four frames are: structural, human resources, political and symbolic (Renso, 2026). A ValleyShield Office would be reporting to an approved UFV Executive. Human-resources frame includes hiring and training specialists. Politically, UFV's 2026-27 budget anticipates a $23 million decline in international tuition revenues, along with an 8.9% decline in consolidated revenues in its year-over-year budget table (UFV, 2026). Departments may have conflicts over space, funds, and authority, but new revenue may be a help.

The symbolic frame is a university where they're getting ready for a crisis, and it's easy to see when a campus pilot and annual drill take place. Another reason for stage gates is that UFV’s willingness to take risks in a commercial venture is untested. I wouldn't say that the project has already been approved by UFV. The steering committee would mediate any conflicts regarding resources prior to having them impact project work.

7Champion

A champion champions the project, eliminates obstacles and facilitates resources and approvals. The course emphasises the importance of good management support since a project manager is not the one that makes all decisions (Renso, 2026). The champion would be UFV's President, if formally appointed.

An operational co-champion could be a health-authority representative, following a formal agreement. The champion would not be able to diagnose cases or make public-health orders. I would be bringing major delays and requests for additional funds to the champion rather than having individual teams making such decisions. When UFV and a partner disagree on who should pay costs for shared equipment, I would ask the champion for assistance in resolving the issue prior to delay.

8Deliverables

PathoPulse Lab, 60 wastewater sites, farm and care site monitoring, AI dashboard, Campus Shield pilot report, four surge units, supplies plan, UFV Institute and commercial operating plan are all deliverables. Each should be something that the sponsor can look at and take responsibility for.

The lab must be calibrated and results validated, and the wastewater locations must have useful results—not just installed equipment. Campus Shield should demonstrate trustworthy sample handling, validated energy savings and if its signals provide added value to current information. Engineering inspections and an activation exercise for the surge units need to be conducted. I would have a checklist for each handover in place to ensure work is supported by test results/sign-off.

9Project Life-Cycle Model

I'd probably go with a combination of waterfall and spiral. In the lab, designs, approvals and inspections are planned and happen in an orderly fashion, which is suitable for waterfall. Short cycles of software would involve demonstrations and feedback, including the AI models (Renso, 2026).

The steering committee concludes each phase at a decision point – either the steering committee can continue, make changes, or terminate the work. Expansion would pause if the first 15 wastewater sites do not pass agreed quality tests by mid-2028. Pilots also must get privacy and security clearance for software. This allows the software team to make improvements to the software without unapproved changes to buildings and equipment.

10Five Project Management Process Groups

The sponsor, manager and charter are established during initiation from January 2027 – March 2027. Planning (January-June 2027) establishes the scope, WBS, schedule, budget and risk plans. Executing (July 2027–September 2029) includes purchases, software and training.

Monitoring and controlling is ongoing, via cost tracking, change requests and audits. Closing will occur from October through December 2029, which will include acceptance, handover, final payments, audits and lessons learned. The project manager would ensure that all of the groups had the necessary documents and approvals before they could go any further with the project.

11Pre-Initiation Tasks

Prior to approval, I would estimate scope, time and cost, identify the sponsor and partners, find a project manager, create a business case, and engage with stakeholders and break it down to smaller packages. These are the six 'pre-initiation' tasks in class (Renso, 2026).

12Progress Reports

A status report will indicate the current condition of the project at a certain time, while a progress report will indicate what has been accomplished (Renso, 2026). Workstream leads would report what has been completed, what is next, what is going well and what is in need of assistance each week.

The steering committee would be provided with budget and schedule variances, milestone status, top risks and decisions required, once a month. 13SWOT Analysis

Strengths: UFV as a regional starting point, a connected network, assets with a 2nd use plan, and costs based on public benchmarks when comparable. Weaknesses: 3 year timeframe, new partnerships, need to get commercial lab experience and numerous expenses to be quoted.

Opportunities include healthier buildings, training, energy savings and providing Campus Shield to additional universities in Canada. Threats include delays, cyber, lack of demand, staffing issues, and floods/duplication of existing surveillance. A savings-based contract should be tried out prior to any revenue. The SWOT results I would use when determining which work to test first, and which work can wait.

14Return on Investment and Payback

The benefits and costs are compared in ROI, the payback period is an indication of when the invested money is recovered, and the NPV takes money's time value into account (Renso, 2026). The forecast is based on the discount-rate assumption of 7% and for purposes of comparison, a 3% discount-rate assumption (Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat, 2022). It expects to spend $454.08 million on projects, to generate revenue of $48.3 million in 2030 and $73.1 million in 2033, and to have operating earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA) of $7.6 million and $24.5 million, respectively. With 30 campuses paying an average of $300,000 per year, Campus Shield could be generating $9 million in revenue annually by 2034. It's a sales goal, not a contract.

These estimates are long term and not guaranteed profits. Even with the annual earnings and reserve income projections, the model comes up with approximately $58 million of unrecovered project cash by 2038, with a potential recovery around 2040. Without a final business value, the 2027–2038 NPV is negative, but is positive when a possible continuing-business value is included. That value is unknown and is not cash that has been already earned. This $505.92 million reserve is in addition to the spending of the projects and does not mean that the entire $1 billion has been repaid. Other benefits, e.g. preparedness, are also important but are not sales. I would also take the forecast out for a test drive with not as many campuses paying and a lower reserve return, before trusting it.

15Project Quality Management

Quality means the project satisfies the need for which it was designed, through planning quality, performing quality assurance and controlling quality (Renso, 2026). Quality is defined for Campus Shield by regular sample collection, unambiguous interpretation of the results, reliable documentation and an experienced reviewer.

The false alarm rate and missed alarms should be reported by the pilot for AI models, and the change of AI models must be approved. Six Sigma (define, measure, analyze, improve, control) should be considered if there are repeated sample failures. On failure of a sample I would note what went wrong and make the necessary adjustments to the process and retest prior to rollout.

17Testing and Cost of Quality

Testing includes software functions, integration with the lab, user acceptance and full surge testing. In addition, sample-handling checks and sensor calibration and comparison with reference methods must be performed prior to the use of the signals from Campus Shield.

Cost of Quality is the cost incurred by the company as a result of prevention (training, design reviews), appraisal (audits, test samples), internal failures (rework during development) and external failures (Renso, 2026). After launch if an alert is false or not received, it is an external failure which may destroy trust. I would allocate time to be able to retest, as it can cost less to find faults before launch, than to fix them afterward should a serious fault occur. For instance, if a CampusRelay sample doesn't pass the agreed lab tests, I would do the test again before accepting that pilot.

18Project Human Resource Management

Human-Resource Management involves planning, hiring, training and managing the team (Renso, 2026). ValleyShield requires additional staff such as lab personnel, analysts, programmers, cyber-security experts, building engineers, logistics, trainers and community staff. According to the Job Bank profiles, the median wages for medical laboratory technologists and epidemiologists in BC are $41 and $50.04 per hour, respectively (Job Bank, 2025a, 2025b).

I would recruit key staff positions first and then find people to fill in specific roles as needed for installation and training surges. Campus Shield requires individuals with a knowledge of building controls, sampling and laboratory workflows. It would also outline when each of the jobs is required and who will be training the replacements for the jobs after 2029.

19Motivating People

Intrinsic Motivation = enjoying the work and Extrinsic Motivation = money and praise. According to Maslow, the first needs people have are fair conditions and safety, then belonging and growth, and Herzberg, separates hygiene (pay, equipment) from motivators (achievement, responsibility) (Renso, 2026).

I would reflect on/celebrate successes, provide mentoring and provide staff with clear tasks to own. Trust is developed by small groups and open meetings. I would also encourage staff to address issues early rather than late when they become missed deadlines.

20Resource Planning

Resource planning starts from the WBS and includes individuals, resources (Equipment, buildings, software, etc.), and materials required for each phase of the project (Renso, 2026).

I would use a resource histogram so not more than one expert is engaged on a number of key tasks at the same time. Campus Shield requires: cartridge kits, approved installation sites, transport materials, Controls Engineers, lab capacity. Availability of staff and equipment would be arranged with delivery dates, so equipment is not wasted or left unused. In 2028, building engineers would be required for the UFV pilot; and lab personnel would have to be prepared prior to the arrival of routine samples.

21Communicating to Stakeholders

The communications plan indicates to the groups what is required, who makes it, how it is communicated, when it is required and how issues will be escalated (Renso, 2026). Farmers are looking for clear directions, universities for installation and Privacy updates, and the steering committee for budget decisions.

There would be a notice posted openly on the campus stating what is measured and what isn't, but no personal data. Where necessary, public information will be made available and be multi-lingual. If there are any serious safety or privacy issues, these would be reported directly to the responsible lead rather than waiting for a monthly report.

22Top 10 Risks and Controls

The likelihood, impact, cause, owner and response are recorded in the risk register. The top ten risks I have are those around partner agreements, lab approvals, poor samples, AI mistakes, privacy or cyber issues, surge unit delays, staff issues, revenue shortfalls, community distrust and floods or supply issues. Campus Shield has to be able to demonstrate that it is actually saving money.

For threats, I would avoid, mitigate, transfer or accept; and when pilots work, exploit or enhance opportunities (Renso, 2026). There needs to be one control and one trigger for each high risk, with poor sample results providing a pause and a cyber failure providing a trigger to the incident-response plan. I would look at the top risks each month, and see how their controls were working. Sample-quality risks would be managed by the lab lead, data incidents by the cybersecurity lead and revenue shortfalls by the finance lead.

23Procurement Management

The procurement process involves the decision on the goods to be purchased, the selection of suppliers, control of procurement contracts and contract closure (Renso, 2026). An RFQ is appropriate for a well-defined purchase and an RFP is appropriate when a vendor is required to make a proposal on the design. ValleyShield would purchase lab instruments, wastewater equipment, modular units, cloud based services, sensor equipment, controls integration and training equipment.

I would request the supplier to provide life cycle cost, maintenance, delivery and cyber security and warranty information and not just the price, and would apply documented competition for large contracts. The Campus Shield offer should also meet a standard tender requirement as universities also buy by RFP. Details of delivery dates, acceptance tests, what support would be required and consequences of late delivery would be clearly set out in the contracts.

24Handling Stakeholders

Stakeholder engagement is defined as unaware, resistant, neutral, supportive or leading (Renso, 2026). Facilities, finance and health and safety teams would most likely be the decision makers at universities. Campus Shield's offer is: an improved building readiness and, ideally, less energy consumption; and, if there's a way to measure the savings, payment can be based on that. There will be some who are supportive, and privacy officers and/or students will want answers.

I would have a campus design review and then test one building, measure the results and discuss a longer contract, following normal university purchasing. A savings-based contract should always include a baseline of energy consumption, and a mechanism for partitioning verified energy savings. The older Thompson Rivers University projections demonstrate the viability of pursuing work on building-efficiency, but do not establish the cost-saving ability of Campus Shield (TRU, 2013, 2015). The pilot would test performance, it would not guarantee that no one would be infected. The campus should be able to assess the outcomes of the pilot before making a decision on whether or not to consider a longer contract.

25Closing Process

October 2029 to December 31, 2029 are closing dates. They would also complete acceptance tests, get sponsor sign-off, finalize vendor contracts, reconcile the budget, and transfer the technical records as well as run final drills (Renso, 2026).

Operating business will continue after closure, if approved. The closing report would highlight delivery, delays, risks and be an indicator of whether Campus Shield should be expanded (including to partner universities overseas). I would also sign off on the systems that will be continuing to be maintained once project teams are done.
